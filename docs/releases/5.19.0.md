# Damage Control — 5.19.0

Accepted hits now show a small directional mark beside the player and a short trail of lost health in the existing health bar. A steady corner outline shows the existing 0.75-second recovery window, replacing rapid player flicker and the full-screen red wash. Reduced effects keeps the same readable geometry with a restrained fade.

Shot feedback follows the incoming projectile's actual velocity, including after bounces and portal travel. Other located impacts use their recorded direction. Falls and unlocated damage never invent an attacker. An armor interception or a hit rejected during recovery does not become a damage event. Overkill records only the health actually lost.

## Review the hits

**Watch replay** gains a keyboard-accessible timeline and up to three recent-hit buttons. Scrub the final five seconds or jump to a captured hit to pause on a real frame at or after that event. Labels show the recorded source, health change, amount lost and position in the clip. The range can reach the exact final frame even when the recording ends between hundredths of a second.

Markers are limited to the successful recording's actual time bounds. Asynchronous encoding, missing frames and a room change cannot attach stale damage to a different replay. A directionless or direct-fall ending retains its factual lethal label without inventing an accepted damage amount. Clips without per-hit metadata still support ordinary playback.

**Save clip** exports the replay with its factual hit annotations. Scrubbing and hit jumps are disabled during recording; leaving the viewer, hiding the page or resetting the recording releases the relevant work and resources. Replay pictures retain their existing five-second, frame and memory bounds; hit detail is bounded and remains in memory only.

## Compare a retry

Expand **Damage this room visit** on defeat results to see actual accepted health loss and source totals. The record begins when entering or continuing that room; it does not reconstruct an earlier session or claim to cover the entire run.

After a private room rematch ends, its collapsed breakdown shows the attempt beside the original visit. Different or unknown starting health is identified, and one-form boss practice is distinguished from the original full-room encounter. Those cases show separate totals without an improvement claim. Retry starts a fresh comparison; the original result remains unchanged.

Damage values, recovery duration, enemy timing, gun behavior and movement physics are unchanged. The outline follows the authoritative recovery clock even across an immediate room transition. There is no new Campaign revision, Practice scoring reset, saved damage history or progress migration.
